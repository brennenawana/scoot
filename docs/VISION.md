# Scoot — Vision

## One-liner

A tiny buddy in your menu bar that reminds you to move — and makes you glad it did.

## The problem, honestly stated

Sitting still for hours is bad for you: posture, circulation, mood, focus, long-term
health. Everyone knows this. Knowing has never been the problem. The problem is that
every existing "stand up" reminder is a nag — a notification indistinguishable from
the forty other notifications you dismiss without reading. Nags get muted. Muted apps
get deleted.

Scoot's bet: **the reminder itself must be the thing you like**, not the thing you
tolerate. If the nudge is a moment of delight — a little pixel creature hopping into
the corner of your screen to dance at you — then the healthy habit rides in on the
back of an emotional attachment, not discipline. Discipline churns. Attachment retains.

## The thesis: cute, cool, collectible, anticipated

The products that became cultural phenomena at this scale share a recipe:

- **Tamagotchi**: a creature that is *yours*, that you tend, that has moods. The bond
  is the product.
- **Pokémon**: a spectrum from cute to badass so everyone finds "theirs," plus
  engineered anticipation — the next encounter, the unopened pack. You never know
  what you'll get, and that not-knowing is the engine.
- **The Claude Code buddy (April 2025)**: proof this works on a *developer desktop* in
  2025. A pixel-art companion with dice-roll rarity, living in the terminal. People
  loved it, screenshotted it, and genuinely grieved when it was removed. Nobody asked
  for it. Everybody missed it.
- **CS:GO skins**: cosmetic-only variety can sustain a real economy without ever
  gating features. Identity and taste, not power.

Scoot combines these: a **collectible pixel buddy** with rarity tiers, earned through
the healthy behavior itself, delivered with the polish of a native macOS citizen.

## The core loop (the whole product in five beats)

1. Scoot sits calm in your menu bar. You work.
2. On your interval, your buddy nudges you — a dance in the corner, a chime, a wiggle
   of the menu bar icon. Your choice of style.
3. You get up and move. Scoot notices (you go idle) or you tell it (one click).
4. Your buddy celebrates. Your day's count ticks up. Moving earns **roll tickets**.
5. Redeeming a roll is a pack-opening moment: which buddy will you pull? Rarity
   glow, reveal, confetti. Screenshot it. Share it. Anticipate the next one.

Everything else the app ever becomes must protect this loop.

## Product values (the constitution)

1. **Invisible until helpful.** Scoot is a menu bar citizen, not a window. It should
   feel as inevitable as the Apple logo in the corner — zero dock presence, tiny
   memory, instant launch, native materials. When it appears, it's because it has
   something worth appearing for.
2. **Delight over guilt.** No red badges, no streak-shaming, no sad buddy when you
   ignore a nudge. Missing a movement break is met with a wave, never a frown. Guilt
   mechanics juice short-term metrics and poison the brand of a *health* app.
3. **You can't buy a roll. You can only move for one.** Random rewards are earned
   exclusively through movement. Money buys *deterministic* cosmetics — the exact
   skin or accessory you want — never chance. This keeps the anticipation engine
   fused to the health behavior, and keeps us out of loot-box/gambling territory
   legally and morally.
4. **Free by design.** Every functional feature is free forever. Paid is variety and
   identity — cosmetics, seasonal collections — like skins, never like paywalls.
5. **Privacy is table stakes.** A health-adjacent app watches your idle time; that
   data never needs to leave the machine. Telemetry is aggregate, anonymous,
   plainly disclosed, one toggle to kill.
6. **Experimentation is a core feature, not a phase.** Delight is empirical. The app
   ships with an experiment engine in its skeleton so we can vary nudge styles,
   reveal drama, copy tone — and measure what actually delights, forever.

## Where this goes (the long arc)

The buddy is a *platform for presence on your desktop*. Sequenced deliberately:

- **Now**: movement reminders, done with more charm than anyone.
- **Next**: adjacent time rituals — pomodoro mode, ADHD-friendly focus sessions —
  the same buddy, new jobs. One buddy, many hats (literally: the hats are cosmetics).
- **Later**: a physical desk companion (BLE device the buddy "lives in" when you're
  away), Windows (a native port sharing the art/content pipeline), and collectible
  drops as ongoing cultural beats — seasonal species, collaborations.
- **Eventually**: a local, on-device AI gives the buddy a voice and usefulness —
  a "smart enough" companion that knows your rhythms and helps you steer your day.
  Still local. Still yours. Still cute.

We earn each step only by nailing the previous one. The Tamagotchi didn't launch
with a trading-card game.

## What success looks like

- **Attachment**: users name their buddy within the first session (naming is the
  single cheapest attachment signal we can measure).
- **Health**: move-through rate — the fraction of nudges that end in actual movement
  — is the north-star metric. A viral app that doesn't move bodies has failed.
- **Virality**: pulls get shared unprompted. The rare-drop calendar becomes something
  people talk about. K-factor comes from screenshots, not invite spam.
- **Longevity**: like Pokémon, the collection deepens for years without the core loop
  ever getting more complicated than "move your body, meet your buddy."
